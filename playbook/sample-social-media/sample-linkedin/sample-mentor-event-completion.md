This summer, I had the privilege of serving as a mentor for the AI Summer Camp hosted by the Cal Poly Digital Transformation Hub in partnership with Amazon Web Services (AWS).

Over the course of two back-to-back weeks, we welcomed nearly 100 CSU students and then nearly 100 California CC students. Each camp challenged teams to learn new AI skills, collaborate with peers, and build working solutions to real-world problems submitted by CCC and CSU partners.

As a mentor, I had the chance to:

- Guide student teams through their technical projects, and instructing them of the tools AWS has to offer.
- Share what I’ve learned about generative AI, prompt engineering, and building with AWS.
- Watch students—many brand new to AI—go from learning fundamentals on day one to delivering working demos and polished pitches by the end of the week.

At the beginning when Nicholas Osterbur told me about the camp, I was feeling huge doubts and feelings of imposter syndrome. Even though I am a TA at Cal Poly and work at the DxHub, this seemed like a different beast entirely. I learned to lean into my strengths instead of focusing on my weaknesses, especially letting my wonderful coworkers/mentors pick up the slack where I was lacking.

The energy, creativity, and drive these students brought was inspiring. They reminded me why “Learn by Doing” is such a powerful philosophy. When given the tools and a real challenge, students rise to the occasion in incredible ways!

Huge thanks to DxHub, AWS, and the CCC and CSU partners for making these camps possible, and to the students for showing us just how bright the future of AI is.
