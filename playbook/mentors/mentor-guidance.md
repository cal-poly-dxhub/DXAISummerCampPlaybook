# DX Hub Mentor Introduction Guidelines

## I. Main Goal of Introductions

A. Help students to connect with the mentors before camp starts, they should understand your expertise to build trust and create a collaborative learning environment.
B. Should be around 150 words max 200 (be concise and clear)

## II. Personal Introduction

A. Share name and current role
B. Clearly State that you are a DxHub Member
C. Create an approachable first impression

## III. Role Description

A. Overview of Mentoring responsibilities
B. Emphasize your role in:

1.  Guiding teams in AI tool exploration
2.  Supporting the key AI concepts and frameworks
3.  Provide technical guidance throughout the program
    C. Clarify what your purpose and presence is, sets expectations so that students understand how and when to reach out to you specifically.

## IV. DX Hub Highlight (Tweet-Length Response)

A. Prompt: "What is 1 thing you love about working at the DX Hub?"
B. Format: Max 280 characters (can be playful, honest, or heartfelt)
C. Attempt to showcase the human side of being a mentor, what that means to you, build a rapport and give the participants a peek into the culture of the DX Hub.

## V. Project Showcase

A. What to Include:

1.  Description of a past or current project you have worked on
    B. Include Links like:
1.  Published Web Stories/ Articles
1.  GitHub repository
1.  2/7s documentation (if available)
    C. Highlight your technical experience and offer inspiration, helps to showcase the real world applications of the skills you are teaching

## VI. Photo!

A. Optional but highly encouraged, link a photo of yourself to your introduction so participants can easily recognize you.

## VII. Delivery Channels

A. Once finished your introduction should be around 150-200 words.
B. Slack Community Channels
C. Discord (Mentor Channel)

# Student Engagement Guidelines

## I. Appropriate Topics to Discuss

A. Use of AI tools
B. Frameworks like machine learning pipelines and concepts
C. Technical implementation strategies
D. Best Practices in AI development, ethics, and deployment
E. Keep topics focused on the learning outcomes of the camp

## II. Redirect Guidelines

A. Camp Logistics (e.g., schedules, requirements, deadlines)

1.  Action: Refer students to the camp's FAQ or organizers
    B. Internal Operations (hiring)
1.  Action: Direct students to a staff member for assistance

## III. Communication Standards

A. Response Time

1.  Should spend around an hour a week responding to messages.
2.  Respond to students within 48 hours
3.  For more complex questions send a quick "Got it! I will follow up shortly." (or another form of acknowledgement) and then reply with a full response when ready
    B. Tone and Style
4.  Remain professional but supportive
5.  Use clear, inclusive, and jargon free (if jargon is used explain accordingly) language

## IV. When and How to Escalate

A. Escalate if:

1.  You're asked something outside your expertise
2.  Student Behavior Concerns
3.  Requests that violate program guidelines
    B. How to Escalate
4.  Contact staff member immediately for urgent issues
5.  Provide context, links, screenshots, and student information if needed

## V. Boundaries and Limitations

A. Mentoring Scope

1.  Guide, coach, and question - do not complete or solve solutions
2.  Let students lead the work and make choices
3.  Encourage process over perfection
4.  Focus on learning the process over final outcomes
5.  Not intended to be an "IT" role, you are teaching not solving
    B. Availability
6.  Reasonable response times during business hours
7.  You are not expected to be online 24/7
8.  Quality guidance over quantity of interactions
